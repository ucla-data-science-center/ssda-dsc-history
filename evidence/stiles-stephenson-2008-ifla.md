# Stiles & Stephenson 2008 — the fullest single synthesis of the pre-2014 lineage

Stiles, Jon (Data Archivist, UC DATA, UC Berkeley), and Stephenson, Libby (Data Archivist and
Distinguished Librarian, UCLA). "Disappearing disciplinary borders in the social sciences: Data
acquisition – Access – Management." IFLA Social Science Libraries Section Satellite Conference,
University of Toronto, 6-7 August 2008. (file: `stiles_stephenson.pdf`, 40pp)

Added by Tim 2026-08-09. This is the best single source found in this project so far — a
referenced, scholarly account of UCLA's (and Berkeley's, for comparison) data-services history
across four decades, written by the person who ran the UCLA side of it.

## Corrected/precise timeline (supersedes looser wording elsewhere in this project's evidence)

- **1961**: Political Behavior Archive (PBA) founded by Dwaine Marvick, distributed data from
  ICPR (original member), Roper Public Opinion Research Center, and local holdings. Marvick's own
  unpublished proposal, cited directly: *"Plans for the Development of a Social Science Archival
  Data Library in the UCLA Survey Research Center."* Unpublished proposal, August 1967. (New,
  citable primary-source title/date — didn't exist in this project's evidence before.)
- **1964**: Survey Research Center (SRC) formed at UCLA, housed an "Archival Data Library"; PBA
  merged into it. Neither offered consultative services — reference-room basis only.
- **1974**: **Institute for Social Science Research (ISSR) established**, incorporating the SRC
  and the Archival Data Library. This is the correct founding date for ISSR itself — distinct
  from the archive's own naming (below). Update `dsc-evidence-registry/claims/issr-transfer-1977.yaml`
  to reflect this precisely: ISSR founded 1974, "Social Science Data Archive" name/unit created
  1977 within it.
- **1977**: UCLA ISSR posts a job opening for a Data Archivist; **the newly-named Social Science
  Data Archive, housed within ISSR, is formalized as a service unit** — proposed by then
  Associate Director **Donald Treiman**, who "formalized the 'archival data library' into the
  unit it is today, within the ISSR." This matches and precisely explains the "1977" date already
  independently confirmed three other ways (Tim's 2017 deck, Harvard Dataverse's about page,
  Pawlocki & Stephenson 1988) — all of those were describing this same 1977 formalization event,
  not ISSR's founding.
- **~1980**: UCLA Library decided not to purchase 1980 census tapes; ISSR (per Treiman)
  agreed to acquire them instead. **The 1970 Census tapes were transferred from the Library to
  the ISSR archive**, which took on all support/storage. Real institutional handoff moment —
  numeric-data stewardship moved from Library to Archive in ~1980, the reverse direction of the
  2014 MOU that moved it back. Good bookend for the narrative.
- **1990s**: UCLA Library "began to take a stronger interest in providing access to data as a new
  cohort of librarians was hired." Joint collection policies developed: ISSR archive = raw data,
  Library = value-added tools/published statistics. By this point the ISSR Social Science Data
  Archive had become "the de facto campus-wide data service unit," serving public health,
  education, policy studies, law, and management — not just social science departments.
- **2008** (paper's own present): describes ongoing "multiple-points-of-access" model, informal
  UCLA collaboration between Library and Archive, no full merger yet (that's still 6 years off,
  2014).

## Closes the Dodd/Rowe-Ryan loop from `dodd-1982-library-trends-mrdf.md`

This paper cites the same Rowe & Ryan 1974 paper repeatedly and tells the full story behind it:
UCLA's 1970 census Summary Tape Files (STFs) were the library's first foray into numeric data.
**Mary Ryan**, director of UCLA Library's Public Affairs Service (PAS), purchased the California
census tapes/programs/documentation from DUALabs and placed them with the Campus Computing
Network (CCN); PAS offered only limited orientation/reference, "a service designed for
'do-it-yourselfers.'" By 1980, PAS's numeric-data service was "restricted to parts of the 1970
census... for just a few hours per week." Mary Ryan is the same Ryan co-authoring the 1974
*College & Research Libraries* paper — no longer need to separately track that down, this source
covers it directly (with an additional citation: the paper was also reprinted in *Reader in
Machine-Readable Social Data*, Howard White ed., Information Handling Services, 1977, p.199).

## New named people

- **Howard Freeman** — described as ISSR's "strong leader" at the helm, "responsibility for
  ensuring the longevity of the Institute" (era not pinned down precisely, likely 1970s-80s).
- **Mary Ryan** — UCLA Library Public Affairs Service director, 1970s-80s census-era numeric data
  service, co-author of the underlying Rowe & Ryan 1974 paper.
- **David Nasitir** — Director of Berkeley's IDL&RS, quoted 1973 UNESCO report ("thin end of the
  wedge" re: 1970 census data pulling libraries into MRDF).
- **Marilyn Nasitir** — separate person, cited 1980/1982 *Cataloging and Classification of
  Machine-Readable Data Files* series in *Cataloging and Classification Quarterly*.
- **Harry B.G. Ganzeboom** (Free University, Amsterdam) — co-built the Intergenerational
  Stratification and Mobility File (ISMF) with Treiman and Stephenson, funded by the Netherlands
  Organisation for Scientific Research (NWO), Project H50.293. Cross-national UCLA collaboration,
  not yet documented elsewhere in this project.
