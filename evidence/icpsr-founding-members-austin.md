# ICPSR's actual founding member list (Erik Austin's personal history)

Source: https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/about/history/early-years, pulled 2026-07-31
via Wayback Machine (live page 403s to scripted fetches, same issue noted in
`icpsr-history.md`; capture timestamp 20260617101814). "The Founding and Early Years,"
excerpted from a personal history written by Erik W. Austin, whose ICPSR career spanned
41 years, including service as interim executive director. Austin states up front this is
personal recollection, not "official" institutional history.

## The actual founding member count: 22, not 8

Austin addresses this directly as a known problem: "The 'original' ICPSR member
institutions have been the subject of some dispute over the years. Faculty from more than
two dozen universities 'recalled' that theirs was one of the original members, thus adding
up to more than the iconic 'Michigan plus twenty-one' original members." Using membership
records and contemporary testimony, he lists the confirmed 22:

Michigan, UCLA, University of Chicago, Cornell, University of Florida, Georgetown,
University of Illinois, University of Iowa, University of Kansas, Michigan State,
University of Minnesota, University of North Carolina, Northwestern, University of Oregon,
Princeton, University of Rochester, Vanderbilt, Washington University (St. Louis),
University of Washington, Wayne State, University of Wisconsin, Yale.

**UCLA is on the list.** The founding-member claim itself holds up. The "8" figure on
UCLA's own Dataverse page is simply wrong, not by a little.

## Founding rationale (matches deep-research-report2 already in evidence)

Austin: ICPR was "created in 1962 by political scientist Warren E. Miller as the data
dissemination arm of the American National Election Studies (ANES)... the initial genius
of establishing an effective mechanism for sharing a seminal collection of scientific data
cannot be overestimated. Theretofore, scientific data from all disciplines were jealously
guarded by their developers/creators as private resources."

## Caveat

Austin's essay is a personal recollection published on ICPSR's own site, not a primary
document like the 1962 Memorandum of Organization itself. It's still the strongest source
found so far on the actual founding-member list specifically, since it's explicit about
using membership records to correct a documented pattern of institutions misremembering
their own status.
