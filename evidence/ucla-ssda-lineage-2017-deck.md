# UCLA Data Archive deck (2017-11-20) — the SSDA lineage, first-person

Source: Tim's own "Library Data Archive" slide deck, presented Nov 20, 2017 (to Library
staff, post-2014 acquisition / pre-DSC launch). File: `ucla-data-archive-deck-2017-11-20.pptx`
(25 slides, full text + speaker notes extracted). Tagline on the title slide: "Providing
archive and data services since 1961."

## The UCLA lineage (slide 5 + notes)

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1961 | **Political Behavior Archive** started by political science professor Dwaine Marvick |
| 1962 | Founding member of **ICPSR** |
| 1964 | Joined Survey Research Center under Eve Fielder |
| 1977 | Became the **Social Science Data Archive** under the Institute for Social Science Research (ISSR) |
| 2003/2009 | SRC closes; ISSR shifts to grant support; Archive continues as a solo unit in the Social Science Division |
| 2014 | **Joins the Library** (within DIIT) "to continue and amplify the operations and services of the Data Archive" |
| 2018 | **Creation of the Data Science Center** — "umbrella program to build data science support & data archiving across disciplines" |

This independently corroborates Rice & Southall Table 1.1 ("UCLA Data Services, 1961",
see `rice-southall-2016-ch1.md`) and adds the institutional detail the table lacks. It also
settles two "(verify)" items in `timeline.md`: UCLA's 1961 start and the DSC's 2018 creation.

## What the deck gives the paper

- **The origin story is computational, not just archival (slide 2 notes).** "Origin of data
  libraries in the 60s have as much to do with centralized computing environments on campuses
  as social sciences developing as empirical research." Data sharing emerged from cost
  pressure (surveys were expensive); the inter-archive network "spurred the need for data
  curation, documentation, stable files... Much of our current understanding on how to manage
  data came out of these archives." Direct support for the paper's claim that stewardship
  practice predates and produced RDM.
- **The staffing claim (slides 8, 10 notes).** Circa 1985 the Archive employed "technical
  data staff, statisticians, programmers"; in 1970 the Archive and the Research Library
  jointly hired a programmer/tech consultant for the 1970 Census. Libraries employing
  research software labor is a 50-year-old practice, not an innovation.
- **Continuity across media (slide 9 notes).** Punch cards → tape → CD/DVD → web. "The
  practices, support and experiences around data persist - technical data skills are
  irreplaceable in research." Also: major stats packages (SAS, SPSS, R's lineage) originate
  in the mainframe era the archives inhabited.
- **The full-lifecycle service model existed pre-web (slide 7).** Hypothesis development,
  grant support, collection/analysis/management, long-term preservation - in the Social
  Science Division before the Library acquisition.
- **The cycle argument (slide 16 notes).** "Archives originated and were enabled by advances
  in centralized computing... data at scale and data science techniques are an extension."
  Peer archives made the same move (CISER → HPC support, Odum → data science). The
  archive-to-data-science drift is a sector pattern, not a UCLA quirk.
- **Scale reference point (slide 11 + notes).** SSDA held ~600 curated datasets in 2017;
  ICPSR ~9,000. Curated collections concentrate where staffing does.
- **2017 as the hinge (slides 13-16).** The deck already frames the transition: Stodden on
  computational steps mattering as much as data, reproducibility, and a service plan
  covering consulting, archive/repository services, and education (Carpentries). This is
  §4 territory: the DSC was designed as the connective move the paper argues for.

## Companion deck: DIIT staff meeting, 2017-09-19 (added 2026-07-08)

Two months earlier, same lineage, same tagline ("Providing archive and data services since
1961"). Raw markdown in `inbox/processed/2026-07-08-diit-deck-2017-09-19.md`. What it adds
over the November deck:

- **Jamie Jamison identified**: the deck's intro slide lists Jamie Jamison (PA 3) alongside
  Tim (Director) - this is the "Jamie" the 1970 census-hire memory traces to.
- **The era column**: the history table maps each institutional stage to its storage medium
  (1961-64 mainframe/punch card → 1977 magnetic tape → 80s/90s PC/CD-ROM → 2003+ web). Ready-
  made for the "practices persist across media" argument; the speaker notes say it directly:
  "many entities took it on the chin, but the practices and experiences around data persist."
- **1964 wording differs**: this deck says "Merged with Survey Research Center"; the November
  deck says "joined Survey Research Center under Eve Fielder." Use "joined"; verify before
  using "merged."
- **Infrastructure pointers, 2017 state**: SSDA collections lived on Harvard Dataverse
  (dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/ssda_ucla, ~600 datasets); Data Den named as the UCLA
  Data Repository. Useful for the §4 infrastructure-continuity thread (SSDA → Dataverse →
  today's Dataverse migration work).
- **The 2018 YRL move**: physical relocation into the Young Research Library, est. Winter
  2018 - the last step before the DSC umbrella. The stated goals ("build welcoming and warm
  communities of practice around the Archive's data services") are the DSC mission in draft.
- **Roper 1957 named in Tim's own notes** ("first Roper Center 1957") - the same founding
  cohort date Rice & Southall's Table 1.1 documents, cited independently seven years earlier.
