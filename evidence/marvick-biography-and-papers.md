# Marvick biography and the Marvick Papers collection

Found via targeted search 2026-08-09, following up on the "systematic name/staff search" idea.

## Biography (UC Academic Senate In Memoriam)

`senate.universityofcalifornia.edu/_files/inmemoriam/html/dwainemarvick.htm`

- Born May 22, 1923, Northwood, Iowa.
- Iowa State University 1941; drafted 1943, served in the Army (sharpshooter, trained as
  policeman/hospital administrator/Chinese-speaking cavalry officer), discharged 1946 as
  corporal, finished his political science degree at Iowa State.
- Joined UCLA Political Science Department **1954**. Established the **Political Behavior
  Archive (PBA) in 1961**; through his efforts, UCLA became one of ICPSR's 8 founding members,
  1962. Became professor emeritus 1995.
- Research spanned California, West Africa, India, Germany; "one of the first advocates and
  practitioners of computer applications to political data."
- Died February 25, 1999, complications from a stroke, age 76. Survived by wife Elizabeth
  Marvick and sons Louis and Andrew Marvick.
- Note: this document does not itself discuss the archive's institutional history in detail —
  it's a personal/career memorial, not an institutional source. Cited here for the biographical
  anchor dates (1954 arrival, 1995 emeritus, 1999 death) which weren't documented anywhere else
  in this project.

## The Marvick Papers — a real archival collection, not yet consulted

**Dwaine Marvick papers, UCLA Library Special Collections. 31.5 linear feet, 63 boxes,
1955-1998.** Finding aid: `oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765njp/`

Scope note: "correspondence, computer codebooks, computer data printouts, course materials,
election surveys, field reports, notes, master reprint file, and publications." Known survey
holdings referenced in the finding aid include the Detroit Area Study (1964), Los Angeles County
electoral data (1963), the India National Election Survey (1967, matches the ICPSR 25402 record
already found), and West German elections (1965-1991).

**This is the single most promising concrete lead for closing the pre-1977 gap and finding
Marvick's original founding documents** — including, plausibly, his August 1967 unpublished
proposal "Plans for the Development of a Social Science Archival Data Library in the UCLA Survey
Research Center," cited directly in `stiles-stephenson-2008-ifla.md` but not yet located as a
document. The top-level finding aid summary doesn't itemize box/folder contents in enough detail
to confirm it's there — that requires either the full finding aid (not fully fetched) or direct
contact with UCLA Special Collections: spec-coll@library.ucla.edu, (310) 825-4988.

This directly satisfies the README's "UCLA institutional records — University Archives may hold
founding documents..." gap, which was previously speculative ("may hold"). It's now a specific,
named, locatable collection.
