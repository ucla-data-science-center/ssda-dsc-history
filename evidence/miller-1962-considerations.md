# Miller, "Considerations Leading to the Establishment of the ICPR" (1962)

Source: `Miller-Likert.pdf`, downloaded by Tim 2026-07-31. Institute for Social
Research memo, August 1962, from Warren E. Miller (Program Director, Political
Behavior Program) to Rensis Likert (Director, Institute for Social Research). Re:
"The Organization of the Inter-university Consortium for Political Research."

This is a primary founding document, not a later description of one. It predates
or is contemporaneous with the "June 2, 1962 Memorandum of Organization" cited in
`deep-research-report2-intellectual-hist.md`. The two may be related but distinct
documents from the same founding period; the memo itself references "the first
meeting of the Committee," held in Ann Arbor June 1 and 2, 1962, so a separate
Memorandum of Organization from that same meeting is plausible. Treat as an
independent source until the relationship between the two is confirmed.

## The founding member list, dated

"As of July 30, 1962, the participating institutions included": Cornell,
University of Chicago, University of Florida, Georgetown, University of
Illinois, State University of Iowa, University of Kansas, University of
Michigan, Michigan State, University of Minnesota, University of North
Carolina, Northwestern, University of Oregon, Princeton, University of
Rochester, **University of California at Los Angeles**, Vanderbilt, Washington
University, Wayne State, University of Wisconsin, and Yale.

That's 21 institutions, dated and contemporaneous. UCLA is on it.

## This refines, doesn't just confirm, the "8 founding members" correction

`icpsr-founding-members-austin.md` (Erik Austin's later personal-history essay)
counted 22 original members, Michigan plus 21 others. This primary 1962 document
counts 21 total as of July 30, 1962, and Michigan is one of the 21, not counted
separately. The lists mostly match, with one real difference: Austin's list has
both "Washington University (St. Louis)" and a separate "University of
Washington." Miller's contemporaneous list only has "Washington University," no
separate Seattle entry. Possible explanations: University of Washington joined
between July 30 and whenever Austin's cutoff was, Austin's recollection
conflated the two Washingtons, or something else entirely. Not resolved. Doesn't
change the headline finding (the "8" figure is wrong either way), but the exact
number should be cited as "21, per Miller's July 1962 list" or "22, per
Austin," not flattened into one unqualified number.

## No mention of Marvick

The memo never names any individual UCLA faculty member, or any faculty member
from any institution. That's expected, not a gap: the memo's first organizing
principle is explicit that the Consortium was built on "institutional rather
than individual participation" (see next section). So this document neither
supports nor contradicts Marvick's claimed personal role. It just doesn't speak
to it either way.

## The founding rationale, in Miller's own words

Useful direct quotes for the intellectual-lineage material already on the site:

> "The development of survey research during the last twenty years has given a
> powerful stimulus to the growth of an empirically based social science...
> The rapid increase in scholarly interest in the kinds of data which survey
> research can provide has far outstripped the availability of the survey
> mechanism to the interested scholar. This hiatus has become a matter of
> serious concern to many social scientists."

Four organizing principles Miller lays out for the Consortium:

1. **Institutional rather than individual participation** — individual access to
   skills is "relatively meaningless without subsequent support for and
   facilitation" from a home department; the Consortium ties data access to
   ongoing institutional commitment, not one-off personal favors.
2. **Continuing rather than episodic relationships** — built for long-term,
   cumulative association, not sporadic individual contact.
3. **Facilitation of advanced training in research methods** — training was
   named as a primary purpose from the start, not an add-on: "training in the
   broad array of techniques now associated with the exploitation of the survey
   method will constitute a major interest of the Consortium."
4. **Participation in research planning as well as analysis** — starts with
   secondary analysis of existing archives, but explicitly plans to move
   toward member universities co-planning new studies with Center staff.

## Financing (matches the deep-research report)

$2,500 annual dues per member university into a fund administered by the Survey
Research Center, plus a Stern Family Foundation grant for the organizing period.
Confirms the dues-plus-foundation-support model already documented secondhand.
