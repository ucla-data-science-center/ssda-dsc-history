# DSC-era hosted collections: real, dated, cross-disciplinary scope

Added 2026-08-09 at Tim's direction. Four UCLA Dataverse collections DSC hosts/curates for
external research units, verified live — direct evidence for the "de facto campus-wide" and
now "beyond social science" service scope claims already in `history.qmd`.

## UCLA Law COVID Behind Bars Data Project

Dataverse collection "UCLA Law Behind Bars Data Project," created **2022-01-28**, **1,169
items**. Project itself (uclacovidbehindbars.org): UCLA Law-run, collects and analyzes public
data on COVID-19 in prisons, jails, youth facilities, and immigration detention centers
nationally — case counts, deaths, vaccination rates. No explicit DSC/Library credit on the
project's own homepage (site built by an external design firm, Hyperobjekt), but the Dataverse
collection itself is real, dated, and substantial — a criminal-justice/public-health dataset
hosted through DSC infrastructure, well outside social-science-survey territory.

## Williams Institute (LGBT law and policy research)

Dataverse collection "Williams Institute," created **2021-05-27**, 3 items (small Dataverse
footprint) — but per Tim, this understates the real relationship: DSC's curation/consulting
work supported Williams Institute data reaching **ICPSR** directly, not just UCLA's own
Dataverse. Confirmed live via ICPSR search for "Williams Institute" — 5 real studies:

- **TransPop** (ICPSR 37938, 2016-2018) — "the first national probability sample of transgender
  individuals in the United States." Co-investigators: Walter O. Bockting (Columbia), Jody L.
  Herman (UCLA), Sari L. Reisner (Harvard/Fenway).
- **Generations: A Study of the Life and Health of LGB People in a Changing Society** (ICPSR
  37166, 2016-2019) — five-year longitudinal study across three generational cohorts of
  lesbian, gay, and bisexual adults.
- **Combined Generations Wave 1 and TransPop surveys** (ICPSR 38421) — a merged/reweighted
  dataset for combined-population analysis.
- **Mapping LGBTQ Equality: 2010 to 2020** (ICPSR 37877) — state-level LGBTQ policy tracking,
  ~40 laws/policies across all 50 states + DC + 5 territories.
- Project STRIDE (ICPSR 35525) is a fifth hit but predates the Williams Institute/DSC-era
  connection (2004-2005 data, released 2018) — likely a keyword-match false positive, not
  DSC-curation evidence; don't cite this one for that claim.

This is exactly the kind of nationally-significant dataset DSC's curation/consulting work is
associated with — real, cited, methodologically serious research, not a minor service line.

## Cotsen Institute of Archaeology + DIG

Two related Dataverse collections:
- **Cotsen Institute of Archaeology Dataverse**, created 2020-09-16, **108 items**.
- **DIG Dataverse** ("Digital Information Gateway"), created 2020-09-16, **6,360 items** — by
  far the largest collection DSC hosts, larger than the entire historical SSDA/LAMAS/SCSS/LACSS
  corpus combined (571 items). Description: "an initiative to sustainably archive and provide
  access to data associated with archaeological publications from the Cotsen Institute of
  Archaeology Press... to share digital publications and data and to expand citation practices
  within the cross disciplinary field of archaeology."

Notable: DIG's own stated mission — "expand citation practices" in archaeology — is the exact
same data-citation ethos this project has traced back to Stephenson/Dodd's 1980s bibliographic
control work. Real evidence the "data citation → open data/FAIR" throughline isn't just social
science anymore; it's the same discipline applied to an entirely different field, at a much
larger scale.

## What this adds to the history

These four collections are concrete, dated, verifiable evidence for the "2018-present" section's
claim that DSC's scope broadened deliberately beyond social science — law (COVID Behind Bars),
LGBT policy research reaching ICPSR nationally (Williams Institute), and archaeology at scale
(Cotsen/DIG, 6,360+ items). Worth citing directly in `history.qmd`'s "Continuity, not rupture"
or "A logical evolution" subsections as the concrete cross-disciplinary evidence, rather than
leaving those claims as framing assertions alone.
