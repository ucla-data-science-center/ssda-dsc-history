# Eulau, "Good-Bye to Two Dear Friends: Warren Miller and Dwaine Marvick" (1999)

Source: `good-bye-to-two-dear-friends-warren-miller-and-dwaine-marvick-a-personal-memoir.pdf`,
downloaded by Tim 2026-07-31. Heinz Eulau (Stanford), personal memoir published in *PS:
Political Science and Politics*, June 1999, pp. 278-280. Already cited secondhand in
`deep-research-report2-intellectual-hist.md`; this is the first direct read of the actual
text. Written on the occasion of both men's deaths, four weeks apart: Miller in late
January 1999, Marvick in late February 1999 (stroke). Eulau knew both since the early
1950s and was close to both throughout their careers.

## The friendship predates UCLA and ICPSR by a decade

Eulau, Miller, and Marvick all crossed paths at the University of Michigan's Survey
Research Center in the early 1950s, brought together by sociologist Morris Janowitz.
Marvick, fresh off his Columbia PhD, was a Carnegie Fellow at the Center. Miller was
still finishing his own PhD (with Floyd Allport at Syracuse) and had come to the SRC as
an associate study director on the 1952 election study. Sam Eldersveld, a Michigan
assistant professor, was the fourth in what Michigan's political science chair called
"an unholy foursome." Marvick and Miller then both left Michigan for California in the
mid-1950s: Miller to Berkeley (returning to Michigan in 1956 to run that year's SRC
election study), Marvick to UCLA, where he "took deep Western roots." So the personal
relationship between Marvick and Miller existed a full decade before either the Political
Behavior Archive (1961) or ICPSR (1962), formed in the same building, on the same
research team, under the same mentor.

This is real, documented context for the Dataverse page's "through his efforts, UCLA
was one of the founding members" claim, even though it doesn't prove that specific
sentence. A decade-old personal and professional friendship with ICPSR's actual founder
is a plausible informal channel for UCLA's early institutional membership, separate from
and prior to any formal ICPSR governance role. It upgrades the claim from "unsupported"
to "plausible but still not directly documented" — nobody has found a document that
says "Marvick got UCLA to join because of his friendship with Miller." The friendship is
now documented. The causal claim about UCLA's membership still isn't.

## A more precise ICPSR role than previously found

Eulau: "Dwaine, Warren, and I saw each other a good deal during the early years of the
ICPR. Warren was still the executive director, **Dwaine chaired the organization's
council in 1966-67**." Later: "He was an exemplary chair of the ICPR Council." This is
more specific than the deep-research report's "governing board service from 1964 to
1967" — it confirms he didn't just serve on the board, he chaired it, in 1966-67
specifically. Consistent with, and sharper than, what was already on the site.

Still no evidence of any role in the 1962 founding itself. This memoir's own chronology
places Marvick's ICPSR involvement in "the early years," after founding, matching the
existing finding.

## No mention of the archive at all

This is a detailed, affectionate, career-spanning memoir: Marvick's APSA program chair
role in 1972, his panel-naming contribution to the 1956 APSA meeting, his 1988
interview of Eulau for the APSA oral history project (two days, eight double-sided
tapes, at Marvick's home in LA), his broad intellectual range (Freud, Mannheim,
Durkheim, Weber, Simmel came up in conversation), his wife Elizabeth Wirth Marvick (a
political psychologist in her own right, daughter of Chicago urban sociologist Louis
Wirth), his son Jeff, a ~1990 UCLA conference on Indian federalism he organized. The
Political Behavior Archive, the Social Science Data Archive, and data archiving in
general never come up once. Worth noting as a real absence: a Stanford colleague who
knew Marvick's career in detail, writing his memorial, didn't consider the archive worth
mentioning, or didn't know enough about it to mention it. Doesn't resolve anything, but
it's evidence about how Marvick's own peers remembered him.

## Other biographical facts worth keeping

- Marvick's PhD: Columbia (year not given, but he was "just out of Columbia" when he
  arrived at Michigan's SRC in the early 1950s).
- Died: stroke, late February 1999. Was "regularly showing up in his office" as late as
  mid-January 1999 per a colleague Eulau spoke with.
- Interviewed Eulau for the APSA oral history project in 1988 — this suggests an actual
  APSA oral history collection may include a Marvick-conducted interview, and possibly
  other interviews he conducted. Worth checking as a separate lead: if he interviewed
  others too, those recordings/transcripts might exist in that collection.
- Named the three panel titles for the first APSA panels exclusively devoted to
  political behavior, in 1956, under Lasswell's presidency: "Theoretical Developments,"
  "Methodological Developments," and "Empirical Developments in the Study of Political
  Behavior." Shows he was already a recognized organizer in the field five years before
  founding the archive.
- Continued research interest in India documented independently here (a ~1990 UCLA
  conference on Indian federalism), consistent with the "surveys of the Indian
  electorate" already noted in his finding aid per the earlier deep-research report.
