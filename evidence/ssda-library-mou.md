> **TLDR:** The 2014/2015 MOU transferring the Social Sciences Data Archives (SSDA) to the UCLA Library is DSC's institutional-origin document and a binding lever in the reorg fight — it commits the Library to continue Data Archive operations "within DIIT," transfers 2.0 FTE + ~$245K permanent funds + ~$29K subscription funds, and requires SSD consultation before "substantial changes to operations."

# The SSDA-to-Library MOU (Origin of DSC)

## What it is
A fully executed, binding agreement transferring the Social Sciences Data Archives from the Social Sciences Division (SSD) to the UCLA Library. DSC is the direct successor to this transferred unit. This is the contractual foundation the DSC realignment argument rests on.

## Date reconciliation
The document carries two legitimate dates, which is the source of the "2014 vs 2015" confusion:
- **Drafted/effective:** October 30, 2014; effective November 1, 2014.
- **Executed (signed):** August 2015 — Virginia Steel (University Librarian) 2015-08-04, Alessandro Duranti (Dean of Social Sciences) 2015-08-10.

So citing it as "the 2014 MOU" (effective date) or "the 2015 MOU" (execution date) are both defensible. For a formal record, prefer **"the 2014 MOU (executed August 2015)"** to avoid ambiguity.

## Library commitments (the binding parts)
- Continue career appointments for Stephenson and Jamison with no break in service.
- Continue curation of unique Data Archive items at the current level.
- Continue Data Archive operation, services, and processes **within DIIT** (now DTS, currently subject to OneIT consolidation).
- Provide the technological infrastructure to host software, data, and analysis tools.
- Be open to periodic UCLA-faculty assessments of support/services.
- Offer adequate physical space by July 1, 2017.

## The protective clause (the lever)
> "Should the Library decide not to replace one or both of the vacant positions, **or if there are substantial changes to the operations of Social Sciences Data Archives within the Library**, Library and SSD should meet to discuss and ensure the continuation of College faculty's access to the services."

This makes SSD's access a **contractual commitment**, not a service aspiration — and arguably makes an OneIT-driven reorg of DSC a "substantial change" that triggers a consultation requirement.

## What transferred (financial foundation)
- **2.0 FTE + ~$245,000** permanent 19900 funds (subs 0, 1, 6).
- **~$29,000** sub-3 permanent funds for subscriptions/software: ICPSR ($16,540), Roper ($6,150), UC-Berkeley/SDA ($4,700), Colectica/Algenta ($3,800).
- Foundation gift fund "Data Archive Purchase Fund" (604970/56972), ~$10,477.
- Staff: Elizabeth Stephenson (Librarian), **Jina Jamison — almost certainly Jamie Jamison, current DSC staff**, direct personnel continuity from 2014.

## Why it matters for the 2026 reorg
- DSC = SSDA's successor, so the MOU's commitments follow the unit.
- "Substantial changes" language is a consultation lever in the OneIT conversation.
- The ~$29K subscription base and 2.0 FTE are part of DSC's permanent financial foundation, not discretionary.

## Uncertainty
This summary is drawn from a compiled digest of the MOU, not a re-read of the executed original. Before quoting the protective clause in any formal filing (e.g., the 2026–2028 SOR), verify wording against the signed PDF. Whether an internal Library reorg legally counts as a "substantial change to operations" is an argument, not a settled interpretation.
