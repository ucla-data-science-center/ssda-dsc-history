# Pawlocki & Stephenson 1988 — third independent confirmation of 1977, and Stephenson's timeline extended

Pawlocki, Martin, and Stephenson, Elizabeth. "A Micro Based Tape Information System." *IASSIST
Quarterly*, Fall 1988, pp. 25-40. Authors: "Institute for Social Science Research, University of
California, Los Angeles." (file: `iqvol123pawlocki.pdf`)

Added by Tim 2026-08-09, alongside `iqvol371_4_stephenson.pdf` (Stephenson's 2013 IASSIST Q
piece on Sue Dodd) and `Bull of the Asso Info Sci - 2015 - Doty - Learning to curate.pdf`.

## Confirms 1977, independently, for the third time

Opening line: **"The ISSR Data Archive was established in 1977."** This is now confirmed by
three independent sources: Tim's 2017 memory/deck, the Harvard Dataverse collection's about page
(naming Treiman), and now Stephenson herself, writing contemporaneously as ISSR/Archive staff in
1988 — eleven years after the fact, but eleven years closer than anything else in the evidence
base, and from someone who worked there. Upgrade `issr-transfer-1977.yaml`'s confidence
accordingly.

## Confirms LAMAS and SCSS by name, from a primary 1988 source

"At present, this database contains only the surveys conducted as part of the Los Angeles
Metropolitan Area Surveys (LAMAS) or the Southern California Social Survey (SCSS)." This
independently cross-validates the LAMAS/SCSS series found via Dataverse title search in
`evidence/lamas-scss-lacss-series.md` — two unrelated sources (a 2026 Dataverse metadata pull
and a 1988 internal IASSIST paper) converge on the same two series names.

## Stephenson's documented timeline gets longer

The paper's own bibliography lists two earlier works by Stephenson, extending her documented
activity back to at least 1986:
- Stephenson, Elizabeth A., and Diane Bisom. "Using ORION to CREATE an on-line catalog of data
  archive holdings." IASSIST Conference, Los Angeles, CA, May 21-25, 1986.
- Stephenson, Elizabeth A., and Martin Pawlocki. "Index of machine-readable data files for
  women's studies." IASSIST Conference, Vancouver, B.C., May 19-22, 1987.

Combined with the 2013 IASSIST Q piece (Director of SSDA by 2013) and the Doty et al. 2015
"Learning to Curate" piece (still Director, leading the ICPSR curation project through
2012-2014), **Stephenson's documented tenure now spans at least 1986-2014+** — close to the
entire "37-year gap" era. This is the single strongest answer so far to the "who ran the
archive" question; she isn't the only person, but she's a real through-line, not just a name.

New name: **Diane Bisom**, co-author on the 1986 paper — another SSDA/ISSR-affiliated staff
member not previously documented anywhere in this project.

Further data point (Tim, 2026-08-09, citation only, no document pulled): Stephenson is thanked
in the acknowledgments of Deidre Alyse Whitmore's 2016 UCLA dissertation, "Seeking Context:
Archaeological Practices Surrounding the Reuse of Spatial Information" (ProQuest Dissertations &
Theses, document 10125041). Pushes her documented span to **1986-2016+**, and shows the role
included direct research support for doctoral students outside social science proper
(archaeological/spatial data reuse), not just curation and cataloging.

## Operational/technical detail, 1988 snapshot

- Archive ran on UCLA's mainframe (IBM 3090, MVS/VM-CMS) plus a microcomputer-based system
  (dBase III) for tape/administrative records, connected via UCLA's SSCNet token-ring network.
- Bibliographic catalog was accessible via UCLA's campus-wide ORION online library system (the
  Archive maintained its own database within it).
- Used ICPSR's SPIRES-based variable-search facility over CDNet for ICPSR-held files; built its
  own local variable-level database for LAMAS/SCSS (the two series the Archive itself produced
  and didn't get that level of detail from ICPSR).
- Real physical/operational detail: tapes numbered "DTA"/"DTB" prefix + 3-digit sequence,
  archival backup copies kept in a separate location, subject headings up to 20 terms per study,
  policy of filling tape "all but 100 feet."

## Org structure clarified (Stiles & Stephenson 2008, confirmed by Tim 2026-08-09)

SSDA was never an independently incorporated organization before 2014 — it was a service unit
*housed within* ISSR the entire 1977-2014 span (per `stiles-stephenson-2008-ifla.md`). The 2014
MOU is the first point the archive is pulled out of ISSR/SSD and made its own thing, inside the
Library. Before that, "SSDA" essentially means "the archive unit inside ISSR," not a separate
legal or administrative entity.

## Author affiliation detail

Both authors listed under "Institute for Social Science Research" (ISSR), not a separately named
"SSDA" — consistent with the Archive operating as a unit within/under ISSR through at least 1988,
matching the already-established 1977 ISSR-era naming.
