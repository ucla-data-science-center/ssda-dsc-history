# Newsletter scans embedded in the 2017 "Library Data Archive" PowerPoint

Source: `Data Archive .pptx` (11MB), added by Tim to evidence/ 2026-07-31. This is
the original PowerPoint file for the same Nov 20, 2017 deck already transcribed in
`ucla-ssda-lineage-2017-deck.md` ("T. Dennis, 'Library Data Archive,' presentation
to UCLA Library staff, Nov 20, 2017"). The text transcript already existed; the
actual file, with 30 embedded images, did not. Slides 8, 9, and 10 turn out to
contain photographed pages from an actual ISSR-era SSDA newsletter, plus artifact
photos (punch cards, tape cartridge) and a 2017 screenshot of the Dataverse About
page. Extracted images saved to `evidence/newsletter-images/`.

This is the first actual newsletter content recovered for this project. Not a full
digitized issue (these are photographed excerpts arranged in a slide, not a
complete scanned newsletter), but real, dateable, primary-source newsletter text
and staff photos, a first for the 1977-2014 gap.

## Newsletter identification and approximate date

Masthead reads "The Social Science Data Archive: Access to Machine-Readable Data"
(`masthead-ssda-machine-readable-data.png`). One item anticipates "the 1980 Census
data" arriving at "the Data Library," which places this newsletter issue at
approximately **1979**, pre-dating most of what the deep-research report and
Austin's own account had documented for this period.

## Newly named ISSR-era staff, from newsletter photo captions

- **Elizabeth Stephenson, Data Archivist** — caption: "'It must be here
  somewhere.' Elizabeth Stephenson, Data Archivist, looks through the Archive's
  data tape collection." (`stephenson-data-tapes.png`). This is the same Libbie
  Stephenson already documented via her later ICPSR retirement interview and 2007
  award bio (`stephenson-icpsr-sources.md`); this is contemporaneous ~1979
  newsletter confirmation of her working there under her full name, title "Data
  Archivist" at this point, not yet "Director."
- **Martin Pawlocki, Programmer** — caption: "Martin Pawlocki, Programmer,
  maintains and develops the administrative software for the Institute and
  provides assistance to staff and Archive users." (`pawlocki-programmer.png`).
  Not previously documented anywhere in this project's evidence.
- **Gina Jamison, Technical Assistant in the Archive** — caption: "Gina Jamison,
  Technical Assistant in the Archive, will be receiving her Master of Fine Arts
  and leaving ISSR in June." (`jamison-mfa-leaving.png`). **This is the same
  person as Jina/Jamie Jamison**, current DSC staff, per Tim directly: "Gina is
  her proper name" and "she came back to the archive." So her documented
  timeline now has a gap-and-return structure: working at the archive as of this
  ~1979 newsletter, departing around then for her MFA, and returning at some
  later point, continuing through the 2014 Library transfer to the present. The
  "continuous service" language in the 2014 MOU evidently refers to her second,
  longer stint, not one uninterrupted 45+ year tenure. Exact return date still
  unknown.
- **Shirley Lane, Programmer/Technical Consultant** — newsletter text: "ISSR
  welcomes a new Programmer/Technical Consultant, Shirley Lane. Ms. Lane has been
  the consultant for the machine-readable version of the 1970 Census at UCLA's
  Research Library. She joins the staff as part of a cooperative effort between
  the Research Library and the Data Library to provide information and reference
  services utilizing machine-readable data files." (`data-archives-library-relocation.png`).
  Establishes an early, concrete SSDA/UCLA Research Library collaboration on data
  services, and a specific precedent (1970 Census machine-readable version) for
  the archive's work.
- **Charlie Recksieck** — byline on a newsletter item, "Data Archive Seminar,"
  announcing a Feb 9 seminar "The Use of Data Files for the Study of Women" at
  9383 Bunche (`data-archive-seminar-recksieck.png`). Role at the archive not
  stated; likely staff given the byline placement.

## Building/location history

"The Data Archives Library has relocated from Franz Hall to ISSR's new facility
in the Gayley Center Building" (`data-archives-library-relocation.png`). This
adds an earlier location than previously documented: Franz Hall, then the Gayley
Center Building (~1979), predating the "4317 Public Policy Building" (per the
OECD directory, used in the deep-research report) and "11252 Bunche Hall" (per
the 1983-84 General Catalog) locations already on record. The Feb 9 seminar
location, "9383 Bunche," suggests Bunche Hall was already in use for at least
some archive activities even before the catalog's 1983-84 address listing.

## Technical infrastructure, ~1979

Newsletter items describe: tape/cartridge mounting assistance and online-file
access support ("Technical Consulting"); a data-discovery collaboration called
**SPIN**, jointly run with the Center for the Study of Evolution and Origin of
Life (CSEOL), helping researchers find grant/fellowship/travel funding; and
on-line data files hosted on the "SSC 4381 (Datalib)" mainframe, with heavily
used files being migrated to an "OAC 3090" system, including CITIBASE and IMF
data modules for classroom use (`technical-consulting-text.png`,
`spin-grant-funding.png`, `online-data-files-ssc4381.png`).

## Corroborating detail: the "8 founding members" claim is not a recent addition

Slide 23 (`dataverse-page-screenshot-2017.png`) is a screenshot of the live UCLA
Dataverse About page, taken for this Nov 2017 deck. It already reads: "through
his efforts, UCLA was one of the 8 founding members of the Inter-university
Consortium for Political and Social Research (ICPSR) in 1962." This confirms the
unverified "8" figure (see `sources.qmd`'s open-conflicts section) has been on
that page since at least November 2017, not something recently added or edited.
Doesn't change the finding (still unconfirmed, possibly conflated with the
Bentley archive's "Committee of Eight"), just dates how long it's been there.

## What this does and doesn't resolve

Does: gives three new named staff, a more precise ~1979 date anchor, an earlier
building location, resolves the Gina/Jina Jamison identity question (same
person, gap-and-return career), and confirms the "8 founding members" text's
longevity.

Doesn't: this still isn't a full digitized newsletter run, just photographed
excerpts arranged for a 2017 presentation. The `sources.qmd` "Newsletters" item
should be updated to reflect that real newsletter content now exists in evidence,
but the broader physical-location search (University Archives, the newsletters
Tim is checking for the week of 2026-08-03) is still the path to anything more
complete.
