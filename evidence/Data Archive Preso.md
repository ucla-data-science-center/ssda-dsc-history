## What is a data archive? 

Note:
* Social Science data archives have a tradition going back decades (first Roper Center 1957)
* **Archive** implies rigorous long-term preservation procedures (reuse and discovery)
* Some see archives as long-term IT storage service aka long term backup (keeping bits safe)
* The SSDA is more of an Archive in the rigorous long-term preservation sense of archives
* Data Archives should worry about digital preservation concerns, such as, bit rot, format obsolescence, etc.
* Working towards being trusted digital repository standard can give credibility

---

## Role of the Data Archive

* Supports social science research through all stages of a project, including:
  * hypothesis or area of study development
  * grant and funding activities
  * data collection, analysis, management, and communication
  * long term preservation of research data/results/artifacts 
* Provide an inclusive and collaborative environment where researchers explore, learn and work out issues with research data and software.

## Collections

* Approx. [600 datasets](https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/ssda_ucla) (some unpublished, ask us) 
* Mostly from surveys, public opinion polls, and de-identified administrative records. 
* Include studies on:
    * political, social & economic attitudes and behavior 
    * studies of health status and health outcomes
    * international surveys of occupational and social mobility 
    * Los Angeles County Social Surveys & California Polls

## Motivating Quotes for Data Service

>Data is the central currency of science, but the nature of scientific data has changed dramatically with the rapid pace of technology. – Ted Hart (@emhart), et. al., Ten Simple Rules for Digital Data Storage.[doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005097](http://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005097)


Note: 
* This is **objectively** true,  processing digital data is paramount to science, but what does this shift mean for the **modern library**? 
* How do we align our services to support this transition?
* Implied in this quote is the motivation for the discipline of data science (the author is a data scientist for Apple), combining skills in CS, stats and some ability to work with disciplinary data are important skills for research. 

--- 

> Access to the computational steps taken to process data and generate findings is as important as access to data themselves. – Victoria Stodden, Enhancing reproducibility for computational methods. Science 09 Dec 2016 : 1240-1241. [10.1126/science.aah6168](http://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah6168)

Note: 
* How we create and understand data is critical to the precepts of good science - science at it's core needs to be repeatable
* Transparency in research an overarching goal

---

### Last Quote

>[From a survey of] 704 NSF Biological Sciences Directorate principle investigators… **nearly 90%** indicated they are **currently or will soon be analyzing large data sets** ... the most pressing unmet needs are **training in data integration, data management, and scaling analyses for HPC** ... This portends a growing **data knowledge gap** in biology and challenges institutions and funding agencies to redouble their support for **computational training** in biology. - https://doi.org/10.1101/108555

Note: 
* Survey reflect biological sciences, but not hard to **extrapolate** that training is a huge pressing need for all areas to deal with **innovations in research**. 
* The data archive has always provided data instruction to courses and researchers (data literacy)
* As we move forward this will be an increasing feature of the DA suite of services

## Instructional Activities 

![Software Carpentry](http://rse.shef.ac.uk/images/sc-dc-logos.png)  
![Library Carpentry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LibraryCarpentry/librarycarpentry.github.io/master/images/lc-icon-black.png)

Note:
* SWC - Software development best practices (Domain agnostic) - Code modularity, problem decomposition skills, and DRY concepts (writing functions)
* DC - Working effectively with data includes domain-specific content, data organization/management, data lit, data analysis, project based skills
* LC - a bit of both, computational basics, benefits of automation, data 101 -- getting librarians ready to support and then teach researchers on best practices in data and software
* Community of practice model (situated learning) - peer focused
