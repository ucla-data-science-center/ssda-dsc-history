## Correction: she retired in 2016, not 2014

Tim recalled 2014. Two independent details in the retirement-interview article point
to 2016 instead: the article is dated "Jul 8, 2016," and it references an IASSIST
achievement award she'd "recently" received "at the annual conference in Bergen,
Norway" while saying she "officially retires this week." IASSIST's 2016 conference
was confirmed independently (its own conference archive) as held in Bergen, Norway,
May 31 to June 3, 2016. Both details point to the same year. Treat 2016 as the
retirement date going forward, not 2014, unless a better source turns up.

## When she started: ~1977, not fully pinned down but well-triangulated

Two independent primary sources converge on the same year:

- Her own words, 2016: "I became an OR [Official Representative] around 1978... I
  was really green and didn't know very much about data or codebooks."
- The 2007 award citation: her "role as Official Representative for UCLA... has
  spanned 30 years" — 2007 minus 30 is 1977.

Combined with her degree timeline (BA Fine Arts, UC Riverside, 1975; MLS, UCLA,
1977), the most defensible reading is that she started at UCLA's archive around
1977, the same year the archive was formally reconstituted as a named ISSR unit
(December 1977, per `deep-research-report.md`). Not confirmed as a documented
causal link, just a striking, well-supported coincidence: her UCLA tenure appears
to run almost exactly the length of the entire ISSR era, start to Library transfer,
plus two more years.

## Full career profile assembled from these two sources

- BA, Fine Arts, UC Riverside, 1975
- MLS, UCLA, 1977
- Started at UCLA / became ICPSR Official Representative: ~1977-78
- IASSIST President: 1995-1997
- ICPSR Council member: 1996-2000
- 2007: William H. Flanigan Award for Distinguished Service as an ICPSR Official
  Representative
- Wrote "The Role of the Official Representative in the 21st Century" for the
  ICPSR Bulletin, late 1990s
- Helped create the ICPSR OR Blog
- 2016: received an IASSIST achievement award (shared with colleague Ann Green) at
  the Bergen conference, shortly before retiring
- Retired: 2016, after roughly 38-39 years at UCLA

## Why this matters for the site

She is, by a wide margin, the single best-documented and most continuous witness
to the entire ISSR era available. Her tenure runs from right at the archive's 1977
formal establishment through the 2014 Library transfer and two more years,
retiring in 2016, two years before the 2018 DSC rebrand. Unlike Marvick (died
1999) or the ISSR directors (Freeman, Brewer, Sears, all elderly if living), she
retired only a decade ago and is very likely reachable for oral history now. She
should be treated as the top oral-history priority on the candidate list, not one
name among many.
