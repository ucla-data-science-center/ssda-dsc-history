# UCLA’s Social Science Data Archive under ISSR, 1977–2014

## Executive findings

The public record is substantially richer than the single sentence currently in the draft, although it remains uneven. The strongest recoverable outline is:

**The Social Science Data Archive was established as an ISSR service unit in December 1977; it supported quantitative and especially secondary analysis, acquired major public datasets through UCLA’s ICPSR membership and government agencies, preserved UCLA-generated surveys, and supplied data, documentation, reference help, and later research-data-management and curation services.** Official UCLA catalogs consistently listed SSDA as an ISSR component through at least 2005–07. By 2009–10, UCLA had shortened the parent unit’s name to the **Institute for Social Research (ISR)**. ISR still included SSDA in 2010–11, but the institute disappeared from the 2011–12 General Catalog while SSDA continued to be listed independently as a social-science statistical collection. citeturn21view4turn21view0turn13search1turn13search4turn23search0

The publicly documented ISSR directors are **Howard E. Freeman** in the late 1970s and around 1980, **Marilynn B. Brewer** by 1983, and **David O. Sears from 1993 through 2008**. Other identifiable staff include assistant directors Ann Cinderella, Madeleine/Madelyn De Maria, and Tana Wong; statistical-services head Rita Engelhardt; sampling specialist Donald Witzke; programming staff Matthew Futterman and Jack Katola; coding supervisors Cheryl Groves and Kathleen O’Kane; Survey Research Center director Eve Fielder; CATI director Michael Greenwell; and SSDA head archivist/director Libbie Stephenson. citeturn21view7turn23search7turn21view2turn21view3turn21view5

## ISSR’s institutional history and current status

### Short answer

ISSR clearly existed as a UCLA organized research unit from the 1970s through the 2000s. It was called the **Institute for Social Science Research** through at least the 2005–07 catalog period. By the 2009–10 General Catalog, UCLA called it the **Institute for Social Research**, or ISR, while retaining essentially the same interdisciplinary mandate and continuing to list SSDA among its components. ISR remained in the 2010–11 catalog, but the 2011–12 and 2013–14 catalogs instead listed SSDA by itself under “Other Collections.” The current UCLA catalog’s organized-research-unit list includes neither ISSR nor ISR. citeturn12search0turn13search1turn13search4turn23search0turn23search1turn0search10

The safest formulation is therefore:

> **ISSR was renamed the Institute for Social Research by 2009–10. ISR ceased to appear as a named UCLA institute between the 2010–11 and 2011–12 General Catalogs, while SSDA continued as a separately listed Social Sciences resource. No public source located in this search establishes the exact formal dissolution date or identifies a single institutional successor.**

That is stronger than saying ISSR simply “shifted toward grant support” or that it “still exists.” Its former name persists in eScholarship’s repository hierarchy and in historical URLs, but an archival repository label is not evidence that the administrative institute remains active. The eScholarship collection does, however, preserve a sizable body of ISSR working papers and subunit publications. citeturn18search16turn18search4

### The institute’s changing composition

The 1977–78 General Catalog described ISSR as an interdisciplinary research and support organization and identified the Survey Research Center as an integral component under director Howard E. Freeman. The catalog passage did not yet name SSDA, which is consistent with the independent account placing the archive’s establishment in December 1977. [UCLA General Catalog 1977–78](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/1950-1999/77-78catalog.pdf) citeturn4search14turn21view4

By 1983–84, the UCLA General Catalog explicitly listed the **Social Science Data Archive** within ISSR and gave the institute’s location as 11252 Bunche Hall. [UCLA General Catalog 1983–84](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/1950-1999/83-84catalog.pdf) citeturn6search0

The 1994–95 catalog portrayed ISSR as an umbrella for SSDA, the Organizational Research Program, the Center for Social Theory and Comparative History, and survey-research training. [UCLA General Catalog 1994–95](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/1950-1999/94-95catalog.pdf) citeturn6search2turn7search10

The 2003–05 catalog gave a still larger set of components: the Center for American Politics and Public Policy, Center for the Study of Urban Poverty, Center for Research in Society and Politics, Center for Social Theory and Comparative History, Survey Research Center, Social Science Data Archive, and Organizational Research Program. It also said ISSR conducted the annual Los Angeles County Social Survey and published *ISSR Working Papers in the Social Sciences*. Elsewhere in the same catalog, the “Institute for Social Science Research Data Archive Library” was described as holding statistical databases for the social sciences. [UCLA General Catalog 2003–05](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/03-05catalog.pdf) citeturn21view0turn21view1

The 2009–10 catalog changed the heading to **Institute for Social Research**, retained the same 310-825-0711 telephone number, used the website `isr.ucla.edu`, and continued to include SSDA among a group of research centers and programs. The 2010–11 catalog did the same. [UCLA General Catalog 2009–10](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/09-10catalog.pdf); [UCLA General Catalog 2010–11](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/10-11catalog.pdf) citeturn13search1turn13search4

By contrast, the 2011–12 catalog no longer presented either ISSR or ISR as an institute. It listed the **Social Science Data Archive**, with `dataarchives.ss.ucla.edu`, as a collection containing statistical databases for the social sciences. [UCLA General Catalog 2011–12](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/11-12catalog.pdf); [UCLA General Catalog 2013–14](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/13-14catalog.pdf) citeturn23search0turn23search1

## Published references and evidence of SSDA’s work

### Short answer

Public sources show that SSDA was not merely a passive storage room. It was a campus research-support and data-curation unit that acquired external datasets, archived UCLA-produced surveys, distributed data to researchers beyond UCLA, maintained documentation, helped users locate and interpret data, supported teaching, and—by the early 2010s—worked across the research-data lifecycle, including data-management planning, de-identification, preservation, metadata, and long-term reuse. citeturn21view4turn3view0turn3view2turn17search1turn17search17

### Contemporaneous descriptions

An international directory published through the OECD described the **ISSR Data Archive at UCLA** as established in **December 1977** to support quantitative research, “primarily secondary analysis,” within ISSR and the wider UCLA social-science community. It said data came primarily through UCLA’s ICPSR membership, supplemented by the National Center for Health Statistics, Census Bureau, National Center for Education Statistics, Bureau of Labor Statistics, and surveys conducted by ISSR—including the Los Angeles County Social Survey. It also identified the archive as a member of the International Federation of Data Organizations. [OECD, *Social Sciences for a Digital World*](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2000/07/social-sciences-for-a-digital-world_g1gh2541/9789264082137-en.pdf) citeturn21view4

A UCLA research-resources page described “Data Archives” as maintaining machine-readable data files and documentation, helping researchers locate data and documentation, and operating from 4317 Public Policy Building through the old `sscnet.ucla.edu/issr/da/` site. [UCLA OARC research-related resources](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/ucla/research-related-resources/) citeturn17search1

The 2003–05 General Catalog described the archive’s holdings in more library-like language as a collection of statistical databases for the social sciences. That terse catalog description understates the service role documented elsewhere, but it independently confirms continuity and institutional recognition. [UCLA General Catalog 2003–05](https://registrar.ucla.edu/portals/50/documents/catalog-archive/2001-2049/03-05catalog.pdf) citeturn21view1

### Evidence of data distribution and research use

A 1994 item in Donald Treiman’s CV records a codebook for the Social Stratification in Eastern Europe project distributed by the “UCLA, Institute for Social Science Research, Social Science Data Archive.” His CV similarly lists a 1998 Chinese life-history codebook with SSDA as distributor. These entries demonstrate that SSDA disseminated original UCLA-associated comparative survey datasets, not just commercial or government files. [Donald Treiman CV](https://soc.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Treiman-CV.pdf) citeturn15view1turn21view8

A 2007 *American Journal of Sociology* article by Xiaogang Wu and Donald Treiman cited *Life Histories and Social Change in Contemporary China* as distributed by the UCLA Social Science Data Archive and supplied the archive’s ISSR URL. [Wu and Treiman article copy](https://www.ccpr.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Wu-Xiaogang.pdf) citeturn13search3

A 2003 California Center for Population Research paper likewise cited a 1998 codebook as a UCLA Institute for Social Science Research and Social Science Data Archive publication/distribution product. [CCPR paper](https://ccpr.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/The-Household-Registration-System-and-Social-Stratification-in-China_-1955-1996.pdf) citeturn13search6

A 2005 spatial-social-science project reported that an SSDA search tool used geographic and topical selection to retrieve relevant datasets, suggesting that SSDA was developing web-based discovery beyond a simple title list. [Center for Spatially Integrated Social Science final report](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8r80b70b) citeturn1search23

### The archive’s mature service model by the early 2010s

In a response to the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, Libbie Stephenson identified herself as SSDA director and said the archive had operated since 1977, served UCLA faculty and students engaged in quantitative research, archived original survey data, provided access to de-identified public-use files, and supported data throughout its lifecycle. The response also characterized UCLA as a particularly heavy user of ICPSR relative to comparable institutions. [SSDA response to OSTP](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/sites/default/files/microsites/ostp/digital-data-%28%23010%29%20UCLA_SSDA.pdf) citeturn3view0turn19search17

A related SSDA response concerning human-subjects protections described users from sociology, political science, economics, public health, law, geography, history, medicine, and nursing, and discussed de-identification and access to reusable data. [SSDA response concerning IRB and data issues](https://dataprivacylab.org/projects/irb/SSDA.pdf) citeturn3view2turn19search21

A 2011 UCLA California Center for Population Research notice said SSDA acted as UCLA’s ICPSR liaison and helped researchers with data-management planning, ICPSR deposits, and reuse. [UCLA CCPR notice on the DMPTool](https://ccpr.ucla.edu/2011/11/02/cdl-data-management-planning-tool/) citeturn1search8

A 2014 data-quality paper treated UCLA SSDA as one of three disciplinary archives used to illustrate data-quality review and curation practices. [Peer, Green, and Stephenson, “Committing to Data Quality Review”](https://isps.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/CommitingToDataQualityReview_idcc14-PrePrint.pdf) citeturn17search17

## Survey Research Center transition and timeline corrections

### Short answer

The best public evidence does **not** support a simple claim that the Survey Research Center “closed around 2003–2009.” A more accurate provisional account is:

> **SRC was incorporated into ISSR in 1976. It remained publicly listed as an ISSR component in the 2003–05 and 2005–07 UCLA catalogs. A published obituary for director Eve Fielder states that the center moved in 2004 to the Health Services Research Division in UCLA General Internal Medicine, where she served as associate director until retiring in 2006. SRC no longer appeared among the components of the renamed Institute for Social Research in the 2009–10 or 2010–11 catalogs.**

This suggests a transfer or migration of the survey operation, with possible catalog lag, rather than a clearly documented closure. citeturn9search10turn21view0turn12search0turn13search1turn13search4

### The 1976–77 distinction

The obituary says SRC was incorporated into ISSR in **1976**, while the OECD directory says the ISSR Data Archive itself was established in **December 1977**. Those dates do not necessarily conflict:

- 1976 may mark the Survey Research Center’s administrative incorporation into ISSR.
- December 1977 may mark the formal establishment or reconstitution of the Data Archive as a named ISSR service unit.

This distinction would improve the history by replacing a single 1977 “takeover” event with a two-step transition. [Eve Fielder obituary](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/latimes/name/eve-fielder-obituary?id=23343907); [OECD directory](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2000/07/social-sciences-for-a-digital-world_g1gh2541/9789264082137-en.pdf) citeturn9search10turn21view4

There is a separate complication in the pre-1977 anchor. UCLA’s current SSDA Dataverse description associates the 1964 Survey Research Center formation with Eve Fielder, while the obituary chronology places her arrival in UCLA survey operations later and says she became SRC’s first director in 1976. Both cannot be accepted literally without qualification. The 1964 creation of SRC may be correct while Fielder’s directorship began later, or the retrospective Dataverse wording may compress separate developments. [Current UCLA SSDA Dataverse](https://dataverse.ucla.edu/dataverse/ssda_ucla); [Eve Fielder obituary](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/latimes/name/eve-fielder-obituary?id=23343907) citeturn23search2turn9search10

### Evidence for SSDA’s standalone continuation

The 2009–10 and 2010–11 catalogs continued to place SSDA inside the renamed ISR but no longer listed SRC among the institute’s research components. By 2011–12, the institute itself disappeared from the catalog while SSDA remained listed under “Other Collections,” using its own `dataarchives.ss.ucla.edu` website. The same independent listing continued in 2013–14. That is good independent evidence for the claim that SSDA survived the end or dispersal of the parent institute as a standalone Social Sciences resource. citeturn13search1turn13search4turn23search0turn23search1

What remains unproven publicly is the exact administrative chain—whether SSDA reported directly to the Social Sciences dean, to a divisional administrative office, or through another center between ISR’s disappearance and the Library MOU. The catalogs establish visibility and continuity but not reporting lines, budget authority, or personnel supervision.

### Recommended wording for the public history

A citable provisional paragraph would be:

> UCLA established the Institute for Social Science Research Data Archive in December 1977 as a service unit supporting quantitative research and secondary analysis. The Archive acquired data through UCLA’s ICPSR membership, federal statistical agencies, and locally produced studies such as the Los Angeles County Social Survey. It remained one of ISSR’s named components through the mid-2000s. By 2009–10 UCLA had renamed the parent organization the Institute for Social Research; the renamed institute still included the Social Science Data Archive through 2010–11. In the 2011–12 General Catalog, the institute was no longer listed, but the Social Science Data Archive continued independently as a campus collection of statistical databases for the social sciences. citeturn21view4turn21view0turn13search1turn13search4turn23search0

A separate sentence could handle SRC:

> The Survey Research Center followed a partly separate path: although it was incorporated into ISSR in 1976 and remained listed in ISSR’s catalog description into the 2000s, a published account states that it moved to a health-services-research unit in UCLA General Internal Medicine in 2004 rather than simply closing. citeturn9search10turn21view0

**Confidence for this question: 87/100 — strong evidence for a 2004 move and later disappearance from the institute’s catalog listing, but no formal transfer or closure document was found.**

## Evidence gaps and conclusions

The public web can now support more than a placeholder sentence for 1977–2014. It can document a service mission, institutional evolution, several directors, a substantial operational staff network, concrete datasets and research outputs, and the archive’s movement toward modern data curation. The strongest defensible periodization is:

| Period | Publicly supportable characterization | Confidence |
|---|---|---:|
| **1976–77** | SRC incorporated into ISSR in 1976; ISSR Data Archive established in December 1977 | 88% |
| **Late 1970s–1980s** | Archive operated within a broad ISSR infrastructure encompassing survey fieldwork, statistical services, sampling, coding, programming, data acquisition, and secondary-analysis support | 92% |
| **1990s** | SSDA distributed significant UCLA-generated comparative survey datasets; ISSR directed by David Sears beginning in 1993 | 94% |
| **Early 2000s** | ISSR still included SRC and SSDA and conducted the Los Angeles County Social Survey; SSDA had web-based discovery and continued ICPSR and government-data functions | 92% |
| **Around 2004 onward** | SRC reportedly moved into UCLA General Internal Medicine; SSDA remained in the social-science organization | 84% |
| **2009–10** | Parent institute renamed Institute for Social Research; SSDA retained as a component | 94% |
| **2011–14** | ISR no longer appeared in the General Catalog; SSDA continued as a separately listed statistical-data collection before the Library transfer | 91% |

The main unresolved questions are not whether SSDA existed or what broad services it supplied. They are the **exact reporting line after ISR disappeared, the archive’s complete staff succession, the date and authority for the ISSR-to-ISR renaming, the formal disposition of ISR, the precise SRC transfer mechanics, and the internal reasons for the eventual Library move**. Those issues are unlikely to be resolved solely through the currently indexed public web.

The most important timeline complication is that “ISSR era, 1977–2014” may be administratively too simple. Public evidence supports at least three subperiods: **ISSR, 1977 to the late 2000s; renamed ISR through 2010–11; and standalone SSDA from approximately 2011 until the Library transfer.** That distinction should be visible in the finished institutional history rather than treating all thirty-seven years as a single unchanging stewardship arrangement. citeturn21view0turn13search1turn13search4turn23search0turn23search1
