# SSDA @ UCLA — Harvard Dataverse "About" page

Source: https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/ssda_ucla — pulled 2026-07-31, re-pasted with
the full mission/services text 2026-09-02. Institutional self-description of the archive on its
own Dataverse collection page. **Tim confirms 2026-09-02: Libbie Stephenson wrote this text.**
That matters — it is the archive's own account of its history from its longest-serving director
(archive staff/Director ~1977–2016). It is not independent corroboration of anything; it is the
archive's institutional memory.

## Libbie's framing (the key point)

Libbie writes "**The Social Science Data Archive has been operating at UCLA since 1961**" and
treats the archive as one continuous thing — PBA (1961) → the SRC's Archive Data Library (1964)
→ 1977, when "**the research support framework of the Data Archive was designed by Professor
Donald Treiman**" → the 2014 Library reorganization. She does **not** frame 1977 as a founding;
she frames it as a framework redesign of an archive that already existed. She does not mention
ISSR at all. This lines up with Stiles & Stephenson 2008 (which she co-authored): the SRC's
Archival Data Library ran "on a reference-room basis only" until 1977, when it was formalized
into a named unit with a Data Archivist line and consultative services added. **The archive
function is older than the "SSDA" name; 1977 named and formalized it.**

## Full text (as pasted 2026-09-02)

> The Social Science Data Archive has been operating at UCLA since 1961 when political science
> professor Dwaine Marvick established the Political Behavior Archive (PBA); through his efforts,
> UCLA was one of the 8 founding members of the Inter-university Consortium for Political and
> Social Research (ICPSR) in 1962. When the Survey Research Center (SRC) was formed in 1964,
> under the direction of Eve Fielder, the PBA was merged with a planned Archive Data Library the
> SRC was to house. In 1977, the research support framework of the Data Archive was designed by
> Professor Donald Treiman and implemented until it was re-organized into the UCLA Library in
> 2014.
>
> The mission of the Social Science Data Archive has been and continues to be to provide a
> foundation for social science research with faculty support throughout an entire research
> project involving original data collection or the reuse of publicly available studies. Data
> Archive staff and researchers work as partners throughout all stages of the research process,
> beginning when a hypothesis or area of study is being developed, during grant and funding
> activities, while data collection and/or analysis is ongoing, and finally in long term
> preservation of research results. Our role is to provide a collaborative environment where the
> focus is on understanding the nature and scope of research approach and management of research
> output throughout the entire life cycle of the project.
>
> Our Dataverse consists of unique surveys, enumerations, public opinion polls, and de-identified
> administrative records. Topics of studies include political attitudes and behavior; social and
> economic attitudes and behaviors; studies of health status and health outcomes; families,
> children, and women; international surveys of occupational and social mobility; the Los Angeles
> County Social Surveys; California Polls; and, census data for the United States and other
> countries. In addition, the SSDA provides access to data from the Inter-university Consortium
> for Political and Social Research and the Roper Center for Public Opinion Research. Materials
> from SSDA are used with software programs, such as SPSS, SAS, STATA, and R to carry out
> statistical analysis. Assistance in using statistical packages is provided by the IDRE
> Statistical consulting office.
>
> Instructional support, especially support that links research with instruction is also a
> mainstay of operations. The Data Archive is one of the key support components in those
> disciplines that provide instruction in research methods, survey design and methods,
> statistics, and data analysis. Throughout the campus, data provided by the Archive is used by
> faculty in courses to transmit pedagogy, illustrate theory and develop understanding of social,
> political, demographic, economic, geographic and historical trends and issues.
>
> Our Dataverse contains resources to help you find and analyze data, and tutorials to enrich
> your knowledge of data management practices and procedures. We provide advice in preparing data
> management plans and in applying for access to secure or restricted access datasets. [...]

## Where the shaky claims come from

Now attributable: the **"8 founding members"** of ICPSR and **"through his efforts"** (Marvick's
personal role) are Libbie's phrasing here. Miller's actual 1962 memo lists 21 institutions and
gives Marvick no founding role. So "8" and "through his efforts" are the archive's institutional
memory, repeated onto UCLA's Dataverse page and into the 2017 decks — not independent facts. The
**"1964, under the direction of Eve Fielder"** claim also originates here; a published Fielder
obituary says she became SRC's first director in 1976.

## New facts this adds

1. **Donald Treiman** (UCLA sociology professor) designed the "research support framework"
   implemented from 1977 until the 2014 Library reorganization. This is the first named person
   for the 1977–2014 stretch — previously a complete gap in the timeline.
2. **1964 merger detail**: PBA "merged with a planned Archive Data Library the SRC was to
   house." Correction after reading the raw Sept 2017 companion deck (`Data Archive Preso.md`):
   that deck *also* says "Merged with Survey Research Center" — same verb as this page. The
   actual discrepancy is between the Sept 2017 deck + this page (both "merged") and the Nov
   2017 deck, which says "joined Survey Research Center under Eve Fielder." "Merged" looks
   like the older/original term; "joined ... under Eve Fielder" is the outlier to verify.
3. **ICPSR founding-member count**: states UCLA was one of **8** founding ICPSR members.

## Epistemic flag: this page is likely not an independent source

The mission paragraph on this page (services across the research lifecycle — hypothesis
development, grant/funding, data collection/analysis/management, long-term preservation) is
near-verbatim the "Role of the Data Archive" slide in the Sept 2017 companion deck
(`Data Archive Preso.md`). This page is almost certainly maintained by the same team (Tim's)
and recycles that 2017 language rather than being written independently. That means its
historical claims — **Donald Treiman**, **"8 founding members"** — are institutional
self-description, not third-party corroboration, and deserve the same external verification
as any other internal source, not extra weight for being "the current official page."

## Conflicts / things to verify before citing

- **Does not mention ISSR at all.** Both 2017 decks (`ucla-ssda-lineage-2017-deck.md` and its
  companion, `Data Archive Preso.md`) say the archive came under ISSR in 1977, and the
  companion deck names no designer. This page instead credits Treiman with designing the
  framework in 1977, with no institutional-home language. These aren't necessarily
  contradictory — Treiman could have designed the framework *within* ISSR — but the page
  doesn't say so, and two internal sources now agree on ISSR-only framing against this page's
  Treiman-only framing. **Open question: was Treiman an ISSR-affiliated director, or did ISSR
  administratively house a framework designed by faculty
  outside its own leadership?**
- **"8 founding members" of ICPSR is unverified and possibly wrong.** `icpsr-history.md`
  (pulled from ICPSR's own history page) gives 1962-63 membership as 25 institutions but
  doesn't state a "founding" count distinct from that. Commonly cited figures for ICPSR's
  founding cohort in other sources are higher than 8 (double-digit range) — needs a direct
  check against ICPSR's own founding documentation before use. Do not repeat "8 founding
  members" in the public-facing history until verified independently of this self-description.
- **Donald Treiman's availability for oral history is unknown to me and should be verified,
  not assumed either way** — he was a prominent UCLA social-stratification researcher; if he
  is deceased, that changes the oral-history calculus (reinforces urgency for contacting
  anyone who worked with or after him) and shifts research toward his colleagues, students, or
  published papers describing the archive's operations under his framework instead of a direct
  interview.
