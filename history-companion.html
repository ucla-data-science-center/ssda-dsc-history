Full 1977-2014 detail cut from the main narrative

The three subperiods (grounded in the digitized UCLA General Catalogs 2003–05 through 2013–14, per evidence/deep-research-report.md; Sears’s dates independently confirmed): - ISSR proper, 1977 – late 2000s. An OECD international directory dates the “ISSR Data Archive at UCLA” to December 1977, for quantitative research “primarily secondary analysis,” drawing on UCLA’s ICPSR membership plus federal statistical agencies (Census, NCHS, NCES, BLS) and local studies like LACSS. - Institute for Social Research (ISR), ~2009–2011. Parent institute renamed by the 2009–10 UCLA General Catalog, same phone, same components including the Data Archive, new website (isr.ucla.edu). Still in the 2010–11 catalog. No director identified under this name. - Standalone SSDA, ~2011–2014. By the 2011–12 catalog neither ISSR nor ISR appears; SSDA is listed independently as a Social Sciences collection with its own site (dataarchives.ss.ucla.edu). No public source names a formal dissolution of the parent or a single successor. The reporting line from here to the 2014 Library transfer is unresolved. - Immediately pre-transfer (2013), SSDA was piloting the DDI-Lifecycle metadata standard and Colectica curation software. - Southern California Social Survey (SCSS), confirmed running 1984-1991 in the Harvard Dataverse SSDA collection (true start year not pinned down, likely closer to 1977-1980, never deposited in ICPSR at all). - 1988 technical infrastructure detail: mainframe (IBM 3090, MVS/VM-CMS), tape management (DTA/DTB numbering system), UCLA’s ORION catalog, ICPSR’s SPIRES search facility, a microcomputer-based (dBase III) tape-tracking system Pawlocki and Stephenson built.1 - Rotating annual LACSS PIs: Lawrence Bobo (1992, then UCLA Assoc. Professor of Sociology). - Cross-national work in more depth: the Intergenerational Stratification and Mobility File (ISMF), built with Harry Ganzeboom (Free University, Amsterdam) and Donald Treiman, funded by the Netherlands Organisation for Scientific Research (NWO), Project H50.293.2

See evidence/pawlocki-stephenson-1988-iassist.md, evidence/lamas-scss-lacss-series.md, evidence/stiles-stephenson-2008-ifla.md.

Data hosted, 2018-present: full detail

  • UCLA Law Behind Bars Data Project (COVID-19 in prisons, jails, youth facilities, immigration detention), Dataverse collection created 2022-01-28, 1,169 items. Project itself run by UCLA Law; no explicit DSC credit on the project’s own homepage, though the Dataverse collection is real and dated.
  • Williams Institute (LGBT law and policy research), Dataverse collection created 2021-05-27, small footprint (3 items) but DSC’s curation/consulting work supported Williams Institute data reaching ICPSR directly: TransPop (ICPSR 37938, first national probability sample of transgender individuals in the US; co-investigators Walter Bockting, Columbia; Jody Herman, UCLA; Sari Reisner, Harvard/Fenway), Generations (ICPSR 37166, five-year longitudinal LGB health study), the combined Generations/TransPop dataset (ICPSR 38421), and Mapping LGBTQ Equality: 2010 to 2020 (ICPSR 37877).
  • Cotsen Institute of Archaeology (108 items) and DIG, Digital Information Gateway (6,360 items), both Dataverse collections created 2020-09-16. DIG’s stated mission: “expand citation practices” in archaeology.

Full visualization methodology

Holdings-activity bar chart: 571 datasets in the Harvard Dataverse SSDA collection (dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/ssda_ucla), pulled via the collection’s search API 2026-08-09, bucketed by the survey year embedded in each dataset’s title (regex-matched, last 4-digit year 1930-2026 in the title string), not the Dataverse “published” date (uniformly 2015-2018, a migration artifact). This is a distinct, smaller number from the “1,014 legacy Social Science Data Archive datasets” Tim’s own Data Summary says were harvested into UCLA’s own live Dataverse instance, different scope (Harvard-hosted historical sub-collection vs. UCLA’s current live instance), don’t conflate the two counts.

Citation event plot: queried live via browser automation against ICPSR’s per-study “Data-related Publications” search (icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/search/publications?q=STUDY_NO:{n}), 2026-08-09, this endpoint is Cloudflare-blocked to scripted/curl access (confirmed, HTTP 403), which is why browser automation was needed. Checked: all 5 confirmed LACSS study records (36599, 36600, 36598, 36563, 36749) and all 8 confirmed LAMAS waves (36631, 36612, 36611, 36624, 36615, 36614, 36607, 36617). SCSS was searched separately for its own ICPSR presence and found to have none, it was never deposited there, confirmed via a direct ICPSR search for “Southern California Social Survey” (only the 5 LACSS records matched, as background-text mentions, not actual SCSS deposits).

See evidence/icpsr-citation-count-lamas-lacss.md and evidence/lamas-scss-lacss-series.md.

Open discrepancies not yet resolved

  • 1977 is not a founding. Framing mostly settled. Tim, Stephenson’s own Dataverse-page text, and Stiles & Stephenson 2008 all agree: the archive function ran continuously from the 1960s (PBA, then the SRC’s Archive Data Library, “reference-room basis only”); 1977 is when Treiman designed the research-support framework, the unit was named SSDA, and a Data Archivist line and consultative services were added. The sources that “disagree” describe different facets: Stephenson credits Treiman and omits ISSR; the 2017 decks and the OECD directory name ISSR; the published book chapter3 says “part of the Survey Research Center” (which by 1977 was a component of ISSR). Still to check documentarily: whether Treiman’s 1977 framework reported directly to ISSR or to SRC-within-ISSR. Confirm against the 1977–78 UCLA General Catalog.
  • The 1964 SRC arrangement and Eve Fielder: Stephenson’s Dataverse-page text says the SRC formed in 1964 “under the direction of Eve Fielder” and the PBA merged into its planned Archive Data Library. A published obituary (LA Times, via legacy.com) says Fielder became SRC’s first director only in 1976, that SRC moved in 2004 to a Health Services Research division in UCLA General Internal Medicine, and that she was associate director there until retiring in 2006. Either SRC ran from 1964 under someone else, or the account compresses two events. Not resolved against SRC’s own records.
  • The ISSR-era director list and three-subperiod structure (spot-verified 2026-09-02): Sears’s ISSR directorship 1993–2008 is independently confirmed (Wikipedia, UCLA psychology page). The three subperiods are grounded in the digitized UCLA General Catalogs (evidence/deep-research-report.md cites 2003–05 through 2013–14). Freeman’s term (1974–1981) and Brewer’s end (1993, when she left for Ohio State and Sears took over) are firm; still open: Brewer’s start year, any gap 1981–1983, and whether the ISSR-to-ISR rename had a formal date/authority (none found).
  • ISSR-to-ISR rename and ISR dissolution: no date or authority found for either; no director identified for the institute under the ISR name (~2009–2011).
  • The “8 founding members” of ICPSR, and “through his efforts”: both phrasings are from Stephenson’s Dataverse-page text, the archive’s institutional memory, not an independent record. UCLA’s founding-member status is primary-verified (Miller’s 1962 memo). The “8” is not, Austin’s list has 22, Miller’s 21; “8” may be the “Committee of Eight” (1962–63 correspondence in ICPSR’s Bentley archive), unread. Marvick’s personal founding role is undocumented (see the 1961–1964 section).
  • UC Carpentries workshop/learner counts: Tim’s SOPA states “28 workshops… 2,896 learners” for the 2023-2025 review period; his Data Summary states “37 workshops (2022–2025)… 1,495 attendees (2024–2025)” for what may be a different scope (UC Carpentries alone vs. UCLA Carpentries + UC Carpentries combined) and a different date window. The founding/co-founding distinction (UCLA Carpentries 2018, UC Carpentries co-founded 2020) is resolved per Tim 2026-08-09; the workshop counts are not. Use the SOPA’s explicitly-scoped figure if one number is needed publicly.
  • Exact dates for three of Norman Powell’s four gifts (data scientist position, deep learning machine/DSC space, DataSquad seed funding), all undated in every source checked.
  • “The first reorg”: Tim mentioned a first reorganization during the 2018-present span that reduced data-science support capacity. Recorded as a bare fact per the Method/independence note’s discipline; not dated or elaborated, deliberately kept out of the public narrative entirely, not just trimmed, since this project’s whole premise is staying independent of any reorg argument (see history.qmd Methods and limitations).
  • Jamie Jamison’s DB dump / staff dates for 1977-1986, tracked in ucla-data-science-center/dataverse-infrastructure#10, not yet received.
  • The Marvick Papers (UCLA Special Collections, 63 boxes, 1955-1998), not yet consulted for primary founding documents, including Marvick’s own 1967 proposal.
  • Tim’s own 2021 book chapter, “From a Data Archive to Data Science” (Dennis et al., in Herndon ed., Data Science in the Library, Facet, pp.99-109), not yet read; likely already contains a considered account of the 2014-2018 transition that predates and could sharpen everything reconstructed here piecemeal.
