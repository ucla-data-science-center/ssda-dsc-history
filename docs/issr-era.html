The ISSR Era (1977–2014)

NoteAbout this chapter

Most of this chapter draws on a single deep-research pass (evidence/deep-research-report.md) that is heavily cited but not yet independently verified claim-by-claim. Treat names, dates, and titles here as well-sourced leads rather than settled fact until spot-checked against the primary documents it cites.

WarningFour internal sources, four different framings of 1977

Both 2017 internal decks say the archive came under ISSR in 1977. The Dataverse About page names Donald Treiman as the 1977 framework’s designer with no institutional home stated. A later book-chapter draft says the archive was created in 1977 “as part of the Survey Research Center,” no ISSR mention at all. External evidence (UCLA’s 1977–78 General Catalog, via the deep-research pass) supports ISSR as the actual named parent, with SRC as a component under it. That makes the book chapter’s SRC-only framing the likeliest one to need correcting, but confirm it against the catalog directly. Don’t assume it. See Sources & Open Questions.

The archive’s 37 years under this institutional umbrella break into three distinct subperiods, not one continuous arrangement.

ISSR proper (1977 – late 2000s)

An international directory published through the OECD describes the ISSR Data Archive at UCLA as established in December 1977 to support quantitative research, “primarily secondary analysis.” Data came mainly through UCLA’s ICPSR membership, supplemented by federal statistical agencies (Census Bureau, National Center for Health Statistics, National Center for Education Statistics, Bureau of Labor Statistics) and locally produced studies such as the Los Angeles County Social Survey.

Renamed: Institute for Social Research (~2009–2011)

By the 2009–10 UCLA General Catalog, the parent institute’s name had changed to the Institute for Social Research (ISR): same phone number, new website (isr.ucla.edu), same components, including the Social Science Data Archive. No director has been identified for the institute under this name. The renamed institute still appears in the 2010–11 catalog.

Standalone SSDA (~2011–2014)

By the 2011–12 catalog, neither ISSR nor ISR appears as a named institute. The Social Science Data Archive is listed independently, as a Social Sciences collection, using its own website (dataarchives.ss.ucla.edu). No public source identifies a formal dissolution date or a single institutional successor for the parent institute.

Elizabeth “Libbie” Stephenson is identified as SSDA’s head archivist and later director through this period, overseeing collections and reference services. By the early 2010s the archive was moving toward modern research-data curation practice: a 2013 conference paper documents SSDA experimenting with the DDI-Lifecycle metadata standard and Colectica curation software immediately before the Library transfer.

What’s still unresolved

  • The exact reporting line for SSDA between ISR’s disappearance (~2011) and the 2014 Library transfer.
  • Who, if anyone, directed the renamed ISR (2009–2011).
  • The formal date and authority for the ISSR-to-ISR rename, and for ISR’s eventual dissolution.
  • Whether digitized ISSR/SSDA newsletters exist anywhere outside the indexes checked so far: UCLA General Catalog, eScholarship, Internet Archive, HathiTrust, Google Books, UCLA Digital Library, none found. This is a negative search result, not proof none exist.
