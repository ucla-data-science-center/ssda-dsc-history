Joining the Library & the Data Science Center (2014–present)

NoteSource

This chapter draws heavily on a full book-chapter draft already written by DSC staff (evidence/data-sci-libraries_chapter_edits.md, “From a Data Archive to Data Science,” Dennis, Yao, Phan, Allen, Jamison, Daniels, Ali) and the 2014 SSDA→ Library MOU (evidence/ssda-library-mou.md). Most of what follows can be adapted directly from that chapter rather than rewritten from scratch.

The 2014 transfer

The Social Science Data Archive transferred from UCLA’s Social Sciences Division to the UCLA Library via a Memorandum of Understanding, drafted and effective November 1, 2014, and executed August 2015 (Virginia Steel, University Librarian, 2015-08-04; Alessandro Duranti, Dean of Social Sciences, 2015-08-10). The MOU transferred:

  • 2.0 FTE and approximately $245,000 in permanent funds
  • Approximately $29,000 in permanent subscription funds (ICPSR, Roper, UC Berkeley/SDA, Colectica/Algenta)
  • A “Data Archive Purchase Fund” foundation gift (~$10,477)
  • Two staff with continuous service: Elizabeth “Libbie” Stephenson (Librarian) and Jina/Jamie Jamison (Programmer/Analyst), a director and a data archivist per the book chapter

The MOU also included a consultation clause: if the Library later made “substantial changes to the operations of Social Sciences Data Archives,” the Library and the Social Sciences Division were to meet and ensure continuity of faculty access to services.

By the 2010s, SSDA’s narrow service profile (survey research data collection, management, and preservation) no longer matched researchers’ emerging needs across formats (text, image, audio) and disciplines. The move to the Library was framed as a way to modernize services rather than simply relocate an existing one.

Per Tim Dennis, Todd Grappone, then UCLA Library’s AUL for Digital Initiatives and Information Technology, shepherded SSDA’s transfer into the Library. This isn’t yet documented in any source beyond Tim’s own account as a direct participant. Grappone later became University Librarian at UC Santa Barbara in 2025; see People for his fuller career record.

The 2018 rebrand: Data Science Center

In Spring 2018, the unit was formally restructured and renamed the Data Science Center (DSC), with a new mission statement:

The Data Science Center’s mission is to foster a welcoming research community by developing data literacy and foundational coding skills through consulting and education. We support researchers throughout the full data life cycle of activities: planning research projects and data management plans; data acquisition, storage, cleaning and usage; analysis; publication; curation; and preservation.

The DSC retained SSDA’s core services (data literacy, data management, data discovery) while broadening scope to serve any discipline, not only the social sciences. Per Tim, Todd Grappone also coined the “Data Science Center” name and helped design and reorient the 2018 service model, alongside the 2018 book chapter’s co-authors.

The Carpentries

The DSC joined The Carpentries, a volunteer-led data science teaching organization, in 2017 (ahead of the formal 2018 rebrand) to scale instruction with a limited staff. By the time of the book chapter’s writing, Carpentries membership at UCLA had grown to over ten instructors across six departments, including the entire DSC team. Tim Dennis won The Carpentries’ 2018 Community Service Award, cited for teaching over 20 workshops and establishing Library Carpentry as a Lesson Program within the organization.

UCLA Dataverse

Before joining the Library, SSDA’s collections lived on Harvard’s hosted Dataverse instance. In 2018, the DSC stood up a local UCLA Dataverse instance, extending the same repository model (DDI metadata support, tiered curation, license restriction) to the whole UCLA research community. Research groups can sponsor their own branded sub-collections within it. Jina “Jamie” Jamison, whose UCLA service traces back through the 2014 transfer, has led on UCLA Dataverse since: she represented UCLA at the Dataverse Community Meeting 2021 and authored the platform’s preservation documentation.

Staffing growth

The unit grew from two staff at the 2014 transfer to five full-time staff, adding roles in GIS (Spatial Data Science Librarian), computational/data-science facilitation, and machine learning. Most of that growth came from redesigning existing library positions rather than net-new hiring, supplemented by a Carpentries-trained instructor pool and a student program modeled on Carleton College’s DataSquad. Doug Daniels joined the DSC team in 2020 as Emerging Technologies Librarian, folding the Lux Lab’s 3D printing, scanning, and drone mapping/imaging services into the center’s portfolio; he won UCLA’s 2026 Librarian of the Year Award for that work.

A note on scope

This history stops at the DSC’s structure as it stood before 2026. As of publication, the unit’s organizational status is under internal review. This history does not attempt to characterize an unresolved process. It will be revisited once that process concludes.
