Resolved

UCLA’s ICPSR founding-member status

Confirmed against the actual 1962 document, not just a later recollection or a university’s own marketing page. Warren Miller’s own August 1962 founding memo lists “University of California at Los Angeles” among the institutions participating “as of July 30, 1962.” See miller-1962-considerations.md. This does not resolve the exact headcount (see below) or Marvick’s personal role (see below).

Open conflicts

The “8 founding members” headcount: not simply wrong, now genuinely unresolved

This looked settled, then got more complicated instead of less. Two independent counts put the full initial ICPSR membership above 8: Erik Austin’s founding-years essay counts 22 institutions from membership records, and Warren Miller’s own August 1962 memo names 21 directly, dated “as of July 30, 1962.” Both include UCLA. Neither has an “8” anywhere.

But ICPSR’s current director, Maggie Levenstein, independently used the figure “8” during a UCLA visit, not obviously drawing on UCLA’s own page. And ICPSR’s own institutional archive at the Bentley Historical Library holds a folder titled “Committee of Eight, Correspondence and Minutes, 1962-1963,” per its finding aid (umich-bhl-9938-finding-aid.md). A plausible reconciliation: “8” refers to a specific founding committee, not the full 21/22-institution membership roster. Both numbers could be correct, describing two different things. Not confirmed. Nobody has read that folder, and nobody has asked Levenstein what she meant. Either would settle this faster than more inference.

Eve Fielder’s SRC directorship: 1964 or 1976?

Three internal sources say the archive merged into the Survey Research Center in 1964 “under Eve Fielder.” A Fielder obituary cited in the deep-research report says she became SRC’s first director in 1976, the same year SRC was reportedly incorporated into ISSR. Not resolved. Needs a documentary check (SRC records, UCLA Sociology/Political Science department histories, or an earlier obituary detail) before either date gets used as fact in the narrative.

1977’s institutional home: four accounts, four answers

  • Both 2017 decks: the archive came under ISSR.
  • The Dataverse About page: Donald Treiman designed the framework; no institutional home named.
  • The book-chapter draft: the archive was created “as part of the Survey Research Center”; no ISSR mention.
  • External evidence (UCLA’s 1977–78 General Catalog, per the deep-research report): ISSR is the named parent, with SRC as a component under it.

The catalog evidence points toward ISSR being correct and the book chapter’s SRC-only framing being the error. That’s an inference though, not a confirmed correction. Verify against the catalog directly.

Marvick’s claimed ICPSR founding role

Separate question from the headcount above, and also not resolved, though closer than it was. ICPSR’s own first annual report names its initial council members, and Marvick isn’t among them. Neither Austin’s founding-years essay nor Miller’s own 1962 memo mentions him, though Miller’s memo doesn’t name any individual faculty member from any institution, since the Consortium was explicitly built on “institutional rather than individual participation,” so that absence carries less weight than his absence from the council-member list does. What changed: Eulau’s 1999 memorial establishes that Marvick and Miller were close personal friends and research collaborators for a full decade before either the archive or ICPSR existed, together at Michigan’s Survey Research Center in the early 1950s. That’s a real, documented mechanism for “through his efforts” to be true informally, even though no document yet says it directly. His formally documented ICPSR role is governing-board service from 1964, rising to chair of the ICPR Council in 1966-67 (per Eulau), all after founding. The Dataverse page’s “through [Marvick’s] efforts, UCLA was one of the founding members” is now plausible rather than simply unsupported, but still not confirmed by any document found so far.

Donald Treiman’s relationship to ISSR

The Dataverse page credits him with designing the 1977 framework but doesn’t mention ISSR. The deep-research report notes his CV supports depositor and intellectual involvement but not necessarily a formal ISSR title. Whether he was an ISSR-affiliated director or a faculty collaborator outside ISSR’s own leadership structure is unresolved. His living status is also unconfirmed.

Newsletters: partial find, full run still missing

No digitized ISSR/SSDA newsletter or annual-report run has been found in any public index checked (UCLA General Catalog, eScholarship, Internet Archive, HathiTrust, Google Books, UCLA Digital Library). That’s still true. But real newsletter content has now turned up, just not where expected: photographed excerpts from an actual ~1979 SSDA newsletter, embedded in the 2017 “Library Data Archive” PowerPoint Tim added to evidence. Three new staff names, an earlier building location (Franz Hall, then the Gayley Center Building), and a resolved identity question (Gina Jamison of the newsletter is Jina/Jamie Jamison of today’s DSC, confirmed by Tim). See evidence/newsletter-images-2017-deck.md. The physical-location inquiry (UCLA University Archives, former staff, checking the week of 2026-08-03) is still the path to anything resembling a full run.

Tim’s recollection that the newsletters referred to the unit informally as “the Data Archive” is now confirmed by this excerpt: the newsletter’s own masthead reads “The Social Science Data Archive: Access to Machine-Readable Data,” and its items refer to “the Data Archive,” “Data Archives Library,” and “the Data Library” interchangeably. That’s the informal self-identification persisting through the ISSR era, the same thread as the 2017 internal decks’ tagline (“providing archive and data services since 1961”).

How to read confidence notes on this site

Where a claim in the narrative chapters carries a confidence note, it follows this rough scale:

  • Very high: a primary document in hand (CV, MOU, obituary, catalog entry)
  • High: a named, dated claim from a credible source, not yet cross-checked
  • Moderate: plausible, sourced, but only one account exists
  • Disputed / unresolved: multiple sources actively disagree
